functions Scaled Dot-Product Formulation link [1: Vaswani p.4 §3.2.1]
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$

Where $Q, K \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$ represent packed linear projections, and the denominator dampens scale divergence under high dimensionality ($d_k = 64$).

1. Variance Dampening via $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ Scaling

Vaswani et al. (2017)

Standard dot-product attention computes compatibility directly through $q \cdot k = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$. Assuming independent components with mean zero and unit variance, the resulting inner product yields an expectation $\mathbb{E}[q \cdot k] = 0$ but an inflated variance $\text{Var}(q \cdot k) = d_k$.

As dimensionality $d_k$ climbs (e.g., $d_k = 64$ in the standard Transformer baseline), dot products grow proportionally in magnitude. When input values to the final softmax layer become excessively polarized, the derivative $\frac{\partial \text{softmax}(z)_i}{\partial z_j} = s_i(\delta_{ij} - s_j)$ collapses to near-zero values. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ standardizes unit variance across projections, successfully avoiding vanished gradient states during backpropagation.

2. Bidirectional Masking Adaptation in BERT

[2: Devlin p.3 §3.1]

Devlin et al. directly preserves Vaswani’s $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ normalization metric without scaling alterations, but removes causal triangular masks in favor of unconstrained bidirectional self-attention.

BERT Bidirectional Transformation Layer:
$$M_{ij} = 0 \quad \forall i, j \quad \text{yielding} \quad A_{i, j} = \frac{\exp\left(\frac{q_i \cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}{\sum_{l=1}^N \exp\left(\frac{q_i \cdot k_l}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}$$
Unlike autoregressive decoders that apply an upper-triangular mask ($M_{ij} = -\infty$ for $j > i$), BERT allows each token to aggregate full-context contextual variance simultaneously across forward and backward sequences.
